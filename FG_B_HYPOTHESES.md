## Le Page Théorème 1 (VERBATIM, p. 3, translated from French)

> "Si la probabilité p satisfait aux hypothèses (P) il existe un réel α < 1 tel que
> lim_n sup_{x,y ∈ P(R^d), x ≠ y} E[ (d(g_n g_{n-1}…g_1 x, g_n…g_1 y) / d(x,y))^τ ]^{1/n} = ρ
> avec 0 < ρ < 1."

### Hypotheses (P) (VERBATIM, p. 2):

> "(P_1) p est à support compact
> (P_2) Il n'existe pas de sous-groupe d'indice fini de G_p et de puissance extérieure ∧^k R^d (0 < k < d) dans laquelle ce sous-groupe est réductible.
> (P_3) T_p et T_p^{-1} sont contractants au sens suivant: il existe une suite (t_n) ∈ T_p (resp (t'_n) ∈ T_p^{-1}) telle que les suites d'applications projectives associées à (t_n) (resp (t'_n)) dans les espaces projectifs P(R^d) (resp P(R^{d*})) convergent vers un point."

### Hypotheses (typed):

- h_B.LP1.group: ambient G = **SL(d,R)** (matrices d×d, determinant 1). [TYPE (i)]
- h_B.LP1.walk_P1: p with compact support on SL(d,R). [TYPE (ii)]
- h_B.LP1.walk_P2: no reducible finite-index subgroup acting on Λ^k R^d. [TYPE (ii)+(i)]
- h_B.LP1.walk_P3: T_p and T_p^{-1} **proximal/contracting** in projective action. [TYPE (ii)]

### Conclusion C_B.LP1:

- Exponential contraction of projective d-pseudo-distance under random product, with explicit rate ρ < 1.

## Le Page Théorème 6 (VERBATIM, p. 38, Local Central Limit Theorem):

> "Si les hypothèses (P_2^*) ou (P_3^*) sont vérifiées pour toute fonction f continue à support compact dans R × P(R^d) on a
> lim_n sup_{(u,x) ∈ R × S^{d-1}} | √(2π) σ √n · E[f(u + log||g_n…g_1 x|| − nγ, g_n…g_1 x)] − ρ(u/(σ√n)) ∫_{R × P(R^d)} f(t, ŷ) dt ν(dŷ) | = 0"

### Hypotheses (typed):

- h_B.LP6.group: G = SL(d,R), p of class B_∞ (B_∞ ⊃ B_1 + non-arithmeticity / strong recurrence). [TYPE (i)+(ii)]
- h_B.LP6.walk: p has finite higher moments and the cocycle σ(g,x) = log||gx||/||x|| is **non-arithmetic** in the sense of (P_2^*) or (P_3^*). [TYPE (ii)]
- h_B.LP6.stat: stationary ν on P(R^d) (Furstenberg measure). [TYPE (iii)]

### Conclusion C_B.LP6:

- Local CLT for log||g_n…g_1 x||: convergence to Gaussian local density at rate √n. [TYPE (iv) — characteristic-function-style decay of *norm-log distribution*, not stationary measure ν Fourier decay]

## Phase 3 — profinite extension

Could LP machinery extend to (Z/3^n)* via "replace projective space with profinite multiplicative group, replace matrix product with abelian product, replace log||gx|| with a 3-adic valuation v_3"?

- The **transfer operator** P_z f(x) = ∫ e^{z σ(g,x)} f(gx) dp(g) is the load-bearing tool. For Syracuse: σ(g, x) = ? The step is x → x · 2^{-v} mod 3^n (where v ~ Geom(2)); cocycle would be σ(g, x) = -v · log 2 (the "log of multiplier"). But this σ is **only a function of g (not of x)** — degenerate cocycle, just a sum of i.i.d. random variables.
- The transfer operator P_z f(x) = E_v[e^{-z v log 2} f(x · 2^{-v} mod 3^n)] = (E[e^{-z v log 2}]) · (averaging operator on (Z/3^n)*).
- The spectral analysis of P_z thus splits into: (a) MGF of the v-distribution (analytic for |Re z| log 2 < log 2, i.e., |Re z| < 1), (b) **a Markov averaging operator on the finite abelian group** (Z/3^n)*. The latter has spectrum given by **characters χ of (Z/3^n)*** (Plancherel/Pontryagin).
- This is **EXACTLY** what R75/R76/R77 already do — Plancherel decomposition into character coefficients μ̂_n(ξ), with the bilinear pair operator and rate-½ off-diagonal claim. The LP machinery, when stripped of the matrix-product structure and re-instantiated on (Z/3^n)*, **collapses into the existing R75/R76/R77 framework already analyzed**.
- The LP "uniform spectral gap on the imaginary line" (Li Thm 1.3, the spectral gap of P_{a+ib}) **becomes** the question of how |μ̂_n(ξ)| decays in ξ — which is the **closure target itself**.

**Phase 3 finding: STRUCTURALLY_REDUCES_TO_TARGET.** The profinite extension of LP/transfer-operator machinery to (Z/3^n)* is not a tool for proving Fourier decay — it IS the Plancherel-Fourier framework R75-R77, which is what we are trying to bound. Profile-extension here amounts to renaming.

This is MODE_H_CIRCULAR: any non-trivial spectral-gap conclusion of LP-on-profinite IS the polynomial-in-A bound we seek.
