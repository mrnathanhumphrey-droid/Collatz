## §1 — The multiplicative ergodic theorem (Oseledets)

Setup (p. 17):

> "Let (Ω, B, P) be a probability space, where Ω = {ω} is the space of elementary outcomes, B is the σ-algebra of measurable subsets of Ω, and P is a non-negative σ-additive probability measure on the measurable space (Ω, B)."

**Theorem 1.2 — Multiplicative Ergodic Theorem (p. 19):**

> "Let A : Ω → GL(m, R) be a measurable map of the space Ω into the group of invertible m-dimensional matrices. Let us suppose that
>
> (10) ln^+ ‖A(ω)‖ ∈ L_1(Ω).
>
> We set A(n, ω) := A(T^{n-1} ω) · A(T^{n-2} ω) · ⋯ · A(ω). Then:
>
> I. If μ_1(n, ω) ≤ ⋯ ≤ μ_m(n, ω) are the eigenvalues of the matrix (A*(n, ω) A(n, ω))^{1/(2n)}, then with probability 1 the limit
> (11) lim_{n→∞} n^{-1} ln μ_i(n, ω) =: χ_i(ω), i = 1, 2, …, m.
>
> II. a) With probability 1 the limit
> (12) lim_{n→∞} (A*(n, ω) A(n, ω))^{1/(2n)} =: Λ(ω)
>
> Moreover, the eigenvalues of the matrix Λ(ω), taken with their multiplicities, coincide with the numbers e^{χ_i(ω)}.
>
> b) For those ω for which Λ(ω) is a non-degenerate matrix, we have
> (13) lim n^{-1} ln ‖A(n, ω) Λ^{-n}(ω)‖ = lim n^{-1} ln ‖Λ^n(ω) A^{-1}(n, ω)‖ = 0.
>
> III. Let exp α_1(ω) < ⋯ < exp α_s(ω) denote the different eigenvalues of the matrix Λ(ω), and k_1(ω), …, k_s(ω) their multiplicities (in general, s = s(ω); it is possible that α_1 = −∞), and ξ_1(ω), …, ξ_s(ω) the corresponding eigensubspaces of Λ(ω). We set V_0(ω) = {0} and V_i(ω) = ξ_1(ω) ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ξ_i(ω). Then the functions ω → α_i(ω), k_i(ω) are T-invariant, and for any x ∈ V_i(ω) ∖ V_{i-1}(ω) with probability 1
> (14) lim n^{-1} ln ‖A(n, ω) x‖ = α_i(ω) for i = 1, 2, …, s." (p. 19)

**Hypothesis registry for Theorem 1.2 (Oseledets MET):**
- H1: A is a measurable map Ω → GL(m, R), i.e. **finite-dimensional real matrices**.
- H2: Integrability ln^+ ‖A‖ ∈ L_1(Ω).
- H3: A stationary ergodic dynamical system (Ω, P, T).
- H4: The product A(n, ω) is **the LEFT product** A_n A_{n-1} ⋯ A_1 of stationary samples.

**Output:** Lyapunov exponents α_1 < α_2 < ⋯ < α_s (the "different values") with multiplicities k_i, and the **Oseledets flag** V_1 ⊂ V_2 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ V_s = R^m.

> "It is usual to call the numbers α_i(ω) (i = 1, …, s) the Lyapunov indices of the sequence of matrices A(n, ω), and the numbers k_i(ω) (i = 1, …, s) their multiplicities. The set of numbers {α_i, k_i}_{i=1}^s is called the spectrum of the Lyapunov indices of the sequence A(n, ω)." (p. 19)

## §5 — Fundamental theorem on Lyapunov indices

Setup (p. 49):
> "In this section we again denote by A_i(ω) a sequence of independent equally distributed random matrices, i = 1, 2, …, and by ν their distribution on the group G of non-singular matrices. In this section we assume, without always mentioning it specifically, that the following integrability condition holds:
> (1) ∫_G ln^+ ‖A‖ dν(A) < ∞.
>
> Also the conditions of independence and equal distribution are not always indicated. As before, H_ν is a semigroup in G generated by the support of the measure ν."

Functional formula for sums of leading Lyapunov indices (Lemma 5.2, p. 53):

> "Let the algebraic closure of the semigroup H_ν coincide with G, let (μ × ν) be the invariant measure of the chain (2), and (μ_s × ν) the invariant measure of the chain whose phase space is Gr_s × G. Then
> (7) α_1 + α_2 + ⋯ + α_s = ∫∫ σ_s(x, A) dμ_s(x) dν(A),
> where σ_s(x, A) is given by (3)."

**Theorem 5.4 — strict separation of Lyapunov indices (p. 54):**

> "Let A_n(·) be a sequence of independent equally distributed matrices with distribution ν satisfying condition (1), and suppose that the algebraic closure of H_ν coincides with G. Then all the Lyapunov indices of the sequence A(n, ·) are different with probability 1:
> (10) α_s − α_{s+1} > 0, 1 ≤ s ≤ m − 1."

**Hypothesis registry for Theorem 5.4:**
- H1: A_i(·) iid in G = GL(m, R) (or analogous matrix group).
- H2: Integrability ∫ ln^+ ‖A‖ dν < ∞.
- H3: **Algebraic closure of H_ν = G** — the support of ν generates (as a semigroup) a Zariski-dense subset of G.

**Output:** All m Lyapunov indices are distinct (simple spectrum).

## §6 — Generalization to semisimple groups and multiplicity (Kotani's result)

**Theorem 6.6 — generalization to a general subgroup X of G (p. 60):**

> "Let A_i(·), i = 1, 2, …, where A_i(·) ∈ G, be a sequence of independent equally distributed random matrices with a common distribution ν, let H_ν be the semigroup generated by the support of the measure ν, and X the group that is its algebraic closure. Suppose that
> a) ∫_G ln^+ ‖A‖ dν(A) < ∞;
> b) X has the contraction property in S(R^m);
> c) the connected component X_0 of the identity of the group X is irreducible in R^m.
> Then
> I. The leading Lyapunov index α of the sequence A(n, ·) is strictly greater than all the remaining indices (that is, it is simple).
> II. If x ∈ R^m ∖ {0}, then with probability 1
> lim n^{-1} ln ‖A(n, ω) x‖ = α.
> III. α = ∫∫ ln ‖Ax‖ dμ_1(x) dν(A) (here the straight line x ∈ S(R^m) is identified with an arbitrary unit vector contained in it; μ_1 is a measure on S(R^m) invariant with respect to the action of A_i(·))."

**Hypothesis registry for Theorem 6.6:**
- H1: A_i(·) iid in a closed subgroup G of GL(m, R), with X = algebraic closure of support-semigroup of ν.
- H2: log-integrability (same as Thm 5.4).
- H3: X has the contraction property in projective space S(R^m).
- H4: X_0 (identity component) acts **irreducibly on R^m**.

**Theorem 6.1 (semisimple case, p. 56):**
> "If X is a semisimple group and the A_i(·) are independent, …" (gives generalization of Theorem 5.4 strict-separation conclusion).

**Theorem 6.11 — Kotani's theorem on Schrödinger products (p. 54 of body / p. 64 of pdf, Kotani [32]):**

> "If, with probability 1, the process q(x, ω) is not deterministic (that is, knowing the values q(x, ω) for x from the half-axis (−∞, 0] we cannot determine the values for x > 0), then with probability 1
> (2) lim_{x→∞} x^{-1} ln ‖A(x, ω)‖ =: α > 0
> for almost all (with respect to Lebesgue measure on the real line) values of the parameter λ."

Context (p. 64):
> "Thus, Kotani's theorem provides us with an example of a product of stationary random matrices whose leading Lyapunov index is positive for almost all λ (the second index is −α, since det A(·) = 1)."

## What GM provides for Syracuse

The Syracuse renewal Tao recursion has been analyzed (in `result_77_5_d_R_identity_check.md`, `result_77_5_renormalization_step.py`, etc.) to give a **leading-order Lyapunov exponent**:

> λ_Syracuse = log 3 − 2 log 2 ≈ −0.28768 (per the project's standing result; see `C1_TAO_RECURSION_FORM.md` for the recursion form and PADE/WATSON for the multi-spectral picture).

GM Theorem 1.2 directly justifies this: under iid Geom(2) random scaling on (Z/3^n)*, the **scalar** Lyapunov exponent exists and equals λ_Syracuse almost surely.

**What GM does NOT provide:**
- An **operator-level** composition formula. The Oseledets flag V_i and the limit Λ(ω) are matrix-level objects in **finite-dimensional R^m**; they give the asymptotic norm growth but not the spectral measure of the iterated transfer operator.
- Anything on **cross-frequency v ≠ v' coupling** — Oseledets handles iid products, but the conclusion is about asymptotic norm growth of A(n, ω) x for a fixed vector, NOT about an operator that acts on a function space whose modes are indexed by frequency ξ.
- A **profinite analog**. GM works on GL(m, R) and its semisimple subgroups. There is no analog for (Z/3^n)* or its inverse limit Z_3*.
