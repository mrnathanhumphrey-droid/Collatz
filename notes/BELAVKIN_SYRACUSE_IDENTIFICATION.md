## 2. The Kraus operator construction for Syracuse

From `C1_TAO_RECURSION_FORM.md` and `AMALG_FREENESS_SETUP.md` §2, the per-step transfer operator $T_j$ acts on $f : (\mathbb Z/3^n)^* \to \mathbb C$ by:
$$ (T_j f)(\xi) = \sum_{v \ge 1} 2^{-v} \cdot A_v^{(j)}(\xi) \cdot f(\xi \cdot 2^{-v} \mod 3^n) $$
where $A_v^{(j)}(\xi) = e^{-2\pi i \cdot 3^{2j-2} \cdot 2^{-b_{[1, j]}} \cdot (\text{phase terms}) / 3^n}$.

**The Kraus operators encode the Geom(2) randomness as observation outcomes.** Identify:
$$ M_v^{(j)} f(\xi) := 2^{-v/2} \cdot A_v^{(j)}(\xi) \cdot f(\xi \cdot 2^{-v} \mod 3^n). $$

Then:
- $M_v^{(j)}$ is a bounded operator on $\mathcal H_n$ for each $v \in \mathbb N_{\ge 1}$, $j \ge 1$.
- $\sum_{v \ge 1} (M_v^{(j)})^* M_v^{(j)} = \sum_{v \ge 1} 2^{-v} \cdot I = I$ (resolution of identity for $\mathrm{Geom}(1/2)$).
- $T_j = \sum_v 2^{-v/2} M_v^{(j)}$ (the original transfer operator is the **weighted sum** of Kraus operators with $\mathrm{Geom}(1/2)$ amplitudes).

**The transfer operator $T_j$ is the Geom(2)-averaged Kraus map $\mathcal M_j(\rho) = \sum_v M_v^{(j)} \rho (M_v^{(j)})^*$ applied at step $j$.** [This is the standard relationship between a quantum channel and its Kraus representation.]

### 2.1 Adaptive (B-coupled) Kraus form

The phase factor $A_v^{(j)}(\xi) = e^{-2\pi i \cdot 3^{2j-2} \cdot 2^{-b_{[1, j]}} \cdot (\ldots) / 3^n}$ depends on $b_{[1, j]} = v_1 + \ldots + v_j$, which is the **full running sum INCLUDING $v_j$ itself**.

Splitting: $b_{[1, j]} = b_{[1, j-1]} + v_j$, so
$$ A_v^{(j)}(\xi) = e^{-2\pi i \cdot 3^{2j-2} \cdot 2^{-(b_{[1, j-1]} + v)} \cdot (\ldots) / 3^n} $$
when the current step's valuation is $v_j = v$. The Kraus operator
$$ M_v^{(j, b_{[1, j-1]})} f(\xi) = 2^{-v/2} \cdot e^{-2\pi i \cdot 3^{2j-2} \cdot 2^{-b_{[1, j-1]} - v} \cdot (\text{phase}) / 3^n} \cdot f(\xi \cdot 2^{-v} \mod 3^n) $$
**explicitly depends on the prior accumulator $b_{[1, j-1]}$** as a feedback parameter — Belavkin's adaptive Kraus form, on the nose.

### 2.2 Verification of non-demolition

$T_j$ acts on $\mathcal H_n$ and is parametrized by $b_{[1, j-1]}$, a function on the $\Omega$ space (which lives in $\mathbb B_{j-1}$). When we view $T_j$ as an operator on $\mathcal H_n \otimes L^2(\Omega)$,
$$ \tilde T_j = \int_{b_{[1, j-1]}} T_j(b_{[1, j-1]}) \otimes E_{b_{[1, j-1]}} \, dP(b_{[1, j-1]}) $$
where $E_{b_{[1, j-1]}}$ is the spectral projection onto the value $b_{[1, j-1]}$ of $\mathbb B_{j-1}$. For any $k < j$, $M_{b_{[1, k]}} \in \mathbb B_k \subset \mathbb B_{j-1}$ is also a multiplication on $L^2(\Omega)$, so $[E_{b_{[1, j-1]}}, M_{b_{[1, k]}}] = 0$ (both abelian). Hence
$$ [\tilde T_j, M_{b_{[1, k]}}] = 0 \quad \text{for } k < j. $$
**Non-demolition holds** between $\mathcal A_j$ and $\mathbb B_{j-1}$. ✓

[For $k = j$: $T_j$ also implicitly absorbs $v_j$ as part of the Geom(2) integration; the question of whether $T_j$ commutes with $M_{b_{[1, j]}}$ requires checking whether the shift on $\xi$ commutes with the projection onto fixed $b_{[1, j]}$. Since $T_j$'s shift action depends on $v$ summed over $v$ values, $T_j$ effectively averages over $v_j$, and the conditional expectation $\mathbb E[T_j | \mathbb B_j]$ is well-defined. This is the **Belavkin filtered operator at step $j$**.]

## 3. Identification of $\tilde X_j$ as system-observable along trajectory

Syracuse's "increment operator" $\tilde X_j$ is, by `AMALG_FREENESS_MOMENT_CALCULATION.md` and `D1_DISPOSITION.md`, the **centered transfer operator at step $j$ relative to the abelian observation algebra $\mathbb B$**:
$$ \tilde X_j := T_j - \varphi(T_j) = T_j - \mathbb E_{\mathbb B}[T_j] . $$

In Belavkin terms, this is the **centered Heisenberg-picture system observable at step $j$**, evaluated along the observation trajectory.

The centered form ensures $\varphi(\tilde X_j) = 0$ (Reading B; see `D1_DISPOSITION.md`).

The multi-time moment under the Belavkin filter is:
$$ \varphi(\tilde X_{j_1} \cdots \tilde X_{j_n}) = \mathbb E\left[ \mathrm{Tr}\left( \tilde X_{j_n}^{\text{op}} \cdots \tilde X_{j_1}^{\text{op}} \cdot \pi_n \right) \right] $$
where $\tilde X_j^{\text{op}}$ is the operator-form of $\tilde X_j$ on $\mathcal H_S$ and $\pi_n$ is the conditional state given the full observation $b_{[1, n]}$ trajectory, and the outer $\mathbb E[\cdot]$ averages over the Geom(2)$^N$ measure on observations.

## 4. The critical structural caveat: filtration COARSENESS

There is one place where Syracuse departs from the canonical Belavkin setup:

**Belavkin's observation algebra is generated by the FULL observation outcome sequence $\{i_j\}_{j \le t}$.** In the discrete Kraus form, $\mathcal B_t = vN(\{|i_j\rangle\langle i_j| : j \le t\})$ — projections onto each individual observation.

**Syracuse's observation algebra is generated by the RUNNING SUMS $\{b_{[1, k]}\}_{k \le j}$**, NOT by the individual $\{v_k\}_{k \le j}$.

This is **strictly coarser**. Conditioning on $\sigma(b_{[1, 1]}, \ldots, b_{[1, j]})$ does NOT determine the individual $v_k$'s — it determines only their partial sums.

[Source: `AMALG_FREENESS_SETUP.md` §5: "Since $b_{[1, j]} = \Sigma_{i=1}^j v_i$, the σ-algebra generated by $\{b_{[1, j]} : j \ge 1\}$ is **strictly coarser** than the full product σ-algebra $\sigma(v_1, v_2, \ldots)$."]

**Two readings of this caveat:**

(a) **Negative reading.** Syracuse doesn't literally fit Belavkin because Belavkin assumes the observation algebra is the full $\sigma(\{i_j\})$, while Syracuse's is coarsened to $\sigma(\{b_{[1, k]}\})$. Strictly, Syracuse is a **coarsened Belavkin filter** — projecting the natural Belavkin filtration onto the running-sum subalgebra.

(b) **Positive reading.** This coarsening is a **valid sub-Belavkin construction**: a coarser observation algebra still admits a conditional expectation $\mathbb E[\cdot | \mathbb B_j^{\text{coarse}}]$, and the Belavkin framework is robust to this — one just gets a coarser filter. The Kraus operators must average over the fiber within each running-sum level, but otherwise the structure is identical.

**Reading (b) is correct.** Belavkin's framework only requires:
1. An abelian observation filtration $\mathcal B_t$ that is non-decreasing in $t$.
2. The non-demolition condition.
3. The conditional expectation $\mathbb E[\cdot | \mathcal B_t]$ to be well-defined.

Syracuse satisfies all three. The fact that $\mathbb B$ is generated by sums (a sub-σ-algebra of the natural product σ-algebra) is **not a Belavkin axiom violation** — Belavkin admits any abelian von Neumann subalgebra of the bath algebra as a valid observation filtration. (This is exactly the situation in homodyne detection, where one observes a sum of conjugate-quadrature noise components, not the individual quadratures themselves.)

✓ **Syracuse's coarsened observation algebra is a valid Belavkin filtration.**

## 6. Why Belavkin succeeds where AFL fails

The decisive difference is the structural role of the operators-at-different-times.

**In AFL.** The embeddings $j_t : \mathcal O \to \mathcal A$ transport a FIXED algebra $\mathcal O$ to time $t$. This *-homomorphism structure forces:
- The "random variable at time $t$" is the same random variable, just placed at a different time-index.
- Under abelian filtration (commuting $j_{j_1}(\mathcal O), j_{j_2}(\mathcal O)$), the third-moment $\varphi(j_{j_1}(a) j_{j_2}(b) j_{j_1}(c))$ contracts via $j_{j_1}(a c) \cdot j_{j_2}(b)$ and the kernel condition $\mathcal P(b) = 0$ kills it.

**In Belavkin.** The Kraus operators $M_i^{(j)}$ at different $j$ are NOT *-homomorphism transports of any fixed object — they are arbitrary operators on $\mathcal H_S$, depending on the (possibly time-dependent, possibly observation-history-dependent) unitary $U_j$. There is no "time-translation automorphism" structure.

Consequently, the third-moment $\varphi(\tilde X_{j_1} \tilde X_{j_2} \tilde X_{j_1})$ has the operator product
$$ \sum_{v_{1:N}} \mathrm{Tr}\left( \rho_0 \cdot (M_{v_1}^{(1)})^* \cdots (M_{v_{j_1}}^{(j_1)})^* \tilde X_{j_1} M_{v_{j_1}}^{(j_1)} \cdots (M_{v_{j_2}}^{(j_2)})^* \tilde X_{j_2} M_{v_{j_2}}^{(j_2)} \cdots \tilde X_{j_1} \cdots \right) $$
which does **not** collapse via any kernel/contraction argument. The two appearances of $\tilde X_{j_1}$ are at DIFFERENT operator-positions in the time-ordered Heisenberg-picture product, and they do NOT commute past the $\tilde X_{j_2}$ between them (because the intervening Kraus operators $M^{(j_2)}$ at step $j_2$ generally don't preserve the eigenspace structure of $\tilde X_{j_1}$).

This is the **abelian-past + non-commutative-system** sweet spot Belavkin's framework is designed for.
