## 3a. Sub-fit (BC 4.7) — multiplicative subgroup of Z_q^*

**Question:** Does μ̂_n(ξ) = E χ(...) reduce to Σ_{x ∈ H} e_q(ξ x) on a subgroup H < Z_{3^n}^*?

**Two failure points:**

1. **q = 3^n violates the "few prime factors" hypothesis** (verbatim from BC, p. 4: p_i > q^ε ⇒ α_i < 1/ε). For q = 3^n, the only prime is 3 and the exponent is n → ∞. The constraint p_1 > q^ε becomes 3 > 3^{nε}, i.e. n < 1/ε. So **for any fixed ε > 0, BC 4.7 ceases to apply once n > 1/ε**. This is *not* a small-print issue — BC's induction on number-of-prime-factors (proof at lines 784–792 of cochrane_mccarthy.txt) breaks down because the induction base requires p > q^ε, which fails for q = p^n large.

2. **Syracuse's summation domain is not a multiplicative subgroup.** From Phase 1 §4(c): the domain of the Syracuse sum (eq 7.2 of Tao) is the tuple-space (ℕ+1)^n with Geom(2) weights, and the *image* in Z/3^n is the value of 2^{-a_1} + 3 · 2^{-a_{[1,2]}} + … + 3^{n-1} 2^{-a_{[1,n]}} mod 3^n. This image is the *Syracuse offset set* F_n(Geom(2)^n) — its multiplicative structure on (Z/3^n)^* is *not* a multiplicative subgroup. (If it were, Tao's whole §7 renewal-process apparatus would be unnecessary; BC 4.7 would close Prop 1.17 in one line.)

**Could one extract a multiplicative-subgroup substructure?** The natural candidate is the subgroup generated by 2 mod 3^n. Since 2 is a primitive root mod 3^n for all n ≥ 1, ⟨2⟩ = (Z/3^n)^* (the whole unit group of size 2·3^{n-1}). The sum on the *full* (Z/3^n)^* is 0 (orthogonality of characters), so this trivial "subgroup" gives no non-trivial info. Proper subgroups ⟨2^d⟩ with d > 1 give index-d subgroups of size 2·3^{n-1}/d — but Syracuse's tuple-image doesn't sit in any of these naturally.

**Verdict 3a: H_C1_BC_DIRECT does NOT apply.** Twofold structural failure (modulus + domain).

## 3b. Sub-fit (Cochrane 1.2 mixed) — S(χ, g, f, p^m)

**Question:** Does μ̂_n(ξ) — or the inner factor A_v(ξ) of the project-internal recursion, or Tao's f(x, b) of eq 7.4 — admit form S(χ, g, f, 3^n) = Σ_{x=1}^{3^n} χ(g(x)) e_{3^n}(f(x)) with χ multiplicative and f, g rational functions of bounded degree?

**Three levels to check:**

### 3b.i. Full μ̂_n(ξ) (Tao eq 7.2)

μ̂_n(ξ) is an **expectation over Geom(2)^n**, weighted by 2^{-(a_1+…+a_n)}. The summation variable is a tuple **a = (a_1, …, a_n)**, not a single x ∈ Z/3^n. To force it into Cochrane form would require a bijection from the weighted tuple-domain to Z/3^n with the weights becoming a multiplicative character — but **the Geom(2) weight 2^{-a_i} is a multiplicative function of 2^{a_i}, not a multiplicative character on (Z/3^n)^***. Multiplicative characters on (Z/3^n)^* have unit modulus; the weight 2^{-a} has modulus 2^{-a} which decays. So **the weight cannot factor as ρ(x) · χ_mult(x) with χ_mult unimodular and ρ a smooth profile** of the form Cochrane 1.2 contemplates.

### 3b.ii. Tao's f(x, 3) conditional expectation (eq 7.4 applied at b=3)

From Lemma 7.2 of Tao: f(x, 3) = (1/2) χ(5x) + (1/2) χ(7x) = (χ(5x)/2)(1 + χ(2x)). This is **a sum of 2 additive characters at shifted arguments** — a 2-term character sum, NOT a Cochrane mixed-sum. Length 2 is trivially bounded by 1; no Cochrane machinery needed. Generalizing to f(x, b) for b > 3: it's a sum of (b−1)/2-many terms of form χ((2·2^{a_2} + 3) x) over conditionally-allowed a_2 — still **a sum of additive characters with linear phase, NOT a single sum with multiplicative twist of polynomial**.

### 3b.iii. Project-internal A_v(ξ) (R75/R77 abstraction)

Per `result_77_T_lead_spectrum.md` line 14, the one-step recursion is μ̂_{n+1}(ξ) = Σ_v 2^{-v} A_v(ξ) μ̂_n(2^{-v} ξ mod 3^n). Here A_v(ξ) is a **single complex number** at a fixed v (an additive-character value at the level-(n+1) shift induced by a_1 = v). It is not itself a non-trivial Cochrane sum — there is no x running over Z/3^n inside A_v. The "exp-sum" character of the recursion lives in the **outer sum over v ∈ ℕ+1**, weighted by 2^{-v} (geometric, decays). That outer sum has only ~n+O(1) effective terms (Geom(2) tail), so is **a 1-dimensional finite sum, not a complete sum over a prime-power modulus**.

### 3b.iv. The R78 trick: polynomial identification

R78 (`C:/Collatz/result_78.md`) attempted to identify Cochrane's setup by writing (1+3)^u = Σ_{k=0}^r C(u, k) · 3^k mod 3^{r+1}, getting g(u) = c · Σ_{k=0}^r C(u, k) 3^k − 9m·u. The resulting g'(u) had every coefficient with v_3 ≥ 1 → D=0 obstruction in Cochrane Theorem 2 (the **coefficient-extraction theorem**, NOT Theorem 1.2). See Phase 4 disambiguation.

For Cochrane 1.2 / Cor 1.1: even if R78's polynomial-identification step yielded a usable g, the degrees d_1, d_2 of the resulting polynomials would be **r (the level)**, since the truncated binomial expansion has degree r in u. So d_1 + d_2 ≍ r ≍ n. Then Cor 1.1's bound 4.41 · (3^n)^{1−1/(d_1+d_2)} = 4.41 · 3^{n(1 − 1/n)} = 4.41 · 3^{n-1} **which is exactly the trivial bound on a length-3^{n-1} sum** — gives NO saving as n → ∞.

**This is the load-bearing degree-blowup failure.** Cochrane 1.2 requires d_1, d_2 *bounded uniformly* in m = n; for Syracuse the natural polynomial identification gives d_i growing linearly with n. The bound becomes trivial.

**Verdict 3b: H_C1_COCHRANE_MIXED does NOT apply.** Three independent structural failures (no Z/3^n domain at full level; trivial at conditional-expectation level; degree blowup in R78-style polynomial identification).

## 3c. Sub-fit (Heath-Brown Heilbronn) — n^p / p^2 phase

**Question:** Does Syracuse's phase function reduce to n^p / p^2 (or Fermat quotient q(n)/p) modulo 3^n?

From Phase 1, the Syracuse phase function inside Tao's f(x, b) is:

> phase = ξ · 3^{2j-2} · 2^{-b_{[1,j]}} · (2^{a_2} + 3)   mod 3^n

The structure is **ξ · (powers of 3) · (2-adic-inverse) · (linear in 2^{a_2})**. Specifically, the 2^{a_2} term is **a 2-adic exponential in the variable a_2** (geometrically distributed), viewed mod 3^n. This is structurally different from n^p mod p^2:

- HB has phase = (p-th power map applied to n) viewed mod p^2, with the period-p phenomenon (n^p ≡ n^{'p} mod p^2 when n ≡ n' mod p) giving the Fermat-quotient lift.
- Syracuse has phase = (2-adic exponential 2^{-a_2}) viewed mod 3^n, with no analogous lift structure. The "p" in HB's phase **equals** the modulus's prime (p^2 = p · p); in Syracuse, the "2" in 2^{-a_2} is **coprime to** the modulus 3^n.

This coprime-base property means the 2-adic and 3-adic structures decouple — there is no Fermat-quotient lift. Heath-Brown's Stepanov method exploits the algebraic identity n^p − n ≡ 0 mod p; Syracuse has no analogous identity for 2^{-a} mod 3.

**Could a Fermat-quotient analog exist for Syracuse?** The relevant identity would be 2^{φ(3^n)} = 2^{2·3^{n-1}} ≡ 1 mod 3^n. The Fermat-quotient-like object is then (2^{2·3^{n-1}} − 1) / 3^n ∈ Z_3, which is a 3-adic unit. But this is a *fixed* p-adic constant, not a function of summation variable — so there's no "vary n, get a Fermat quotient q(n) varying with n" structure.

**Verdict 3c: H_C1_HEATH_BROWN does NOT apply.** Phase function category mismatch: 2^{-a} mod 3^n (coprime base) vs. n^p mod p^2 (matching base).

## 3d. Sub-fit (Abel summation reduction)

**Question:** Can the non-uniform Geom(2)^n weights be absorbed via partial summation into a sum of pieces fitting 3a/3b/3c?

Abel summation reduces Σ_a w(a) X(a) to a sum involving the partial sums of X(a) weighted by differences of w. For Syracuse:

  μ̂_n(ξ) = Σ_{a_1,…,a_n} ∏_i 2^{-a_i} · χ(2^{-a_1} + 3·2^{-a_{[1,2]}} + … )

Abel summation in a single variable, say a_1, gives:

  μ̂_n(ξ) = Σ_{a_1 ≥ 1} (2^{-a_1} − 2^{-(a_1+1)}) · [partial sum cumulative in a_1]

The partial sum cumulative in a_1 is *still* a sum over (a_2, …, a_n) with the same Geom-tuple structure on those — Abel doesn't change the domain category; it only redistributes weights within the tuple-space. After Abel, the residual sum is *still* over a tuple-space, not over Z/3^n.

**The structural barrier is the tuple-space domain, not the weight non-uniformity.** Abel summation reshuffles weights but cannot change a tuple-space sum into a Z/3^n complete sum. The would-be "residual" of Abel summation is itself another tuple-space sum.

**One more attempt: Fourier inversion to push to Z/3^n.**

Could one write μ̂_n(ξ) as an integral / sum on Z/3^n by Fourier inversion of the tuple-space sum? Yes, but trivially — μ̂_n(ξ) by definition equals Σ_{x ∈ Z/3^n} π_n(x) e^{-2πi ξ x / 3^n} where π_n(x) = Pr(Syrac = x). Then "applying Cochrane" requires π_n(x) to factor as ρ(x) · χ_mult(x) with χ_mult a multiplicative character and ρ a smooth profile / polynomial-of-bounded-degree phase. The Syracuse stationary distribution π_n(x) is **structurally not of this form** — it's an unstructured (in the multiplicative sense) probability distribution determined by the discrete dynamics.

**Verdict 3d: H_C1_ABEL_REDUCTION does NOT apply.** Abel redistributes within the tuple-space; the domain-category mismatch (tuple-space vs Z/3^n complete sum) is not curable by partial summation. Direct Fourier inversion to Z/3^n trivializes the question and exposes the *real* missing ingredient: a structural decomposition of π_n(x) into a smooth-profile times a multiplicative character — which Syracuse's stationary distribution doesn't admit (this is precisely the polynomial-in-A unblocker we'd need to prove, not assume).

## Top-level verdict

**ALL FOUR sub-cases fail.** Disposition: **H_C1_DOESNT_FIT** for Cluster 1 direct attack.

**Failure-mode catalog (which structural feature of Syracuse defeats which theorem):**

| Sub-case | Structural feature of Syracuse that defeats |
|---|---|
| BC 4.7 | (i) q = 3^n violates "few prime factors with p_i > q^ε"; (ii) domain is tuple-space, not multiplicative subgroup of (Z/3^n)^* |
| Cochrane 1.2 | (i) full μ̂_n: weight 2^{-Σa_i} not unimodular multiplicative character; (ii) Tao's f(x, 3): only 2 terms, sub-trivial; (iii) R78-style polynomial identification: degrees d_i ~ n, bound trivial; (iv) iiR78's Theorem-2 D=0 obstruction (see Phase 4) |
| HB Heilbronn | Phase function 2^{-a} mod 3^n (coprime base) vs. n^p mod p^2 (matching base); no Fermat-quotient lift |
| Abel reduction | Tuple-space domain category; partial summation reshuffles within tuple-space, cannot change category to single Z/3^n complete sum |

**The unifying meta-failure: category-of-object mismatch (same meta-pattern as the five-probe arc, restated for arithmetic Cluster 1).** Tao's Syracuse Fourier coefficient is a **product over an n-step 2D renewal walk of conditional expectations of an additive character**, indexed by a Geom(2)^n tuple-space. Cluster 1 theorems target **single complete sums on Z/(p^m) (or its multiplicative subgroups)** with polynomial / rational / Fermat-quotient phase functions. The geometric structure (tuple-product vs single-sum) is incompatible.

**Consistency with R78 + R75/77:** Tao's working strategy is explicitly to **bound the renewal-walk product term-by-term** (via white-point density on the 2D walk), not to apply a single global character-sum bound. The five-probe + R78 + this probe together establish that **the global Cluster-1 character-sum bound is not the right tool for this object** — and Tao's own §7 strategy concedes this implicitly by using the renewal-walk method rather than a direct character-sum bound.
