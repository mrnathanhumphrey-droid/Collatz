## 1. Wiseman 1996 — measurement-operator / Kraus form (the canonical DWM form)

### §2 "Quantum Measurement Theory", p. 4–6:

> "Each r is again associated with an operator Ω_r(T), but it need not be a projection
> operator. I will call these measurement operators. The effect [50] for the result r is defined by
>
>   F_r(T) = Ω_r(T)† Ω_r(T).      (5)"

(p. 4, lines 216–218 of extracted text.)

> "This operator is used to generate the probability for the result via
>
>   P_r(T) = ⟨ψ(t)|F_r(T)|ψ(t)⟩.   (6)
>
> Conservation of probability yields the single restriction which exists for the measurement operators [50], namely
>
>   ∑_r F_r(T) = 1.        (7)
>
> The set of all effects {F(r)} constitutes a positive-operator-valued measure (povm) on the space of results r [50]."

(p. 5, lines 225–231.) — Note: the sum `∑_r` is unrestricted in cardinality, as required for a POVM on an arbitrary measurable space of results r.

> "The state of the system given the result r is
>
>   |ψ_r(t+T)⟩ = Ω_r(T)|ψ(t)⟩ / √P_r(T).    (8)"

(p. 5, lines 232–235.)

> "In terms of the state matrix ρ, which allows the possibility of mixed initial states, the conditioned state is
>
>   ρ_r(t+T) = J[Ω_r(T)]ρ(t) / P_r(T),    (9)
>
> where P_r(T) = Tr[ρ(t) F_r(T)] and for an arbitrary operator a,
>
>   J[a]ρ = a ρ a†.     (10)
>
> The superoperator J[Ω_r(T)] is known as the operation for r [50]."

(p. 5, lines 239–244.)

> "If the measurement were performed but the result ignored, the final state of the system would be
>
>   ρ(t+T) = ∑_r P_r(T) ρ_r(t+T).     (11)"

(p. 5, lines 245–249.)

### §3 "Quantum Trajectories", p. 6 (lines 349–365 of extracted text):

> "One case of the above measurement theory which is of great importance is continuous observation, with an infinitesimal measurement interval T = dt. The form of such a measurement theory is restricted by the requirement that the non-selective evolution generated by the measurements should give a valid evolution equation for the state of the system. Explicitly, this non-selective evolution is
>
>   ρ(t + dt) = ∑_r Ω_r(dt) ρ(t) Ω_r†(dt).    (21)"

This is **the canonical discrete-time Kraus / quantum-trajectory form.** Specifically:
- The set of measurement operators {Ω_r} is in bijection with the result alphabet {r}.
- The sum over r is the operator-sum (Kraus) representation of a CP map.
- The non-selective channel is the unconditional CP transition: `Φ(ρ) = ∑_r Ω_r ρ Ω_r†`.
- The conditional update ρ → Ω_r ρ Ω_r† / P_r is the **Lüders / Davies / quantum-trajectory** state update.

### §6 "Adaptive Measurements", p. 19 (line 798 ff. of extracted text):

> "a real-time adaptive measurement [...]"
>
> "the photocurrent up to time t is used to alter the unitary matrix which determines the measurement operators" (p. 19, lines 803–804.)

Wiseman explicitly admits history-dependent measurement operators Ω_r^{(t, history)} as a canonical extension. (§6 develops this through an explicit adaptive-phase example.)

### Verdict — the canonical DWM Kraus form is `Ω_r(T)` with arbitrary cardinality, conditional updates, and adaptive history-dependence

**The DWM quantum-trajectory form is exactly: a set of measurement operators {Ω_r(T)} on the system Hilbert space with `∑_r Ω_r† Ω_r = I`. The label r ranges over an arbitrary measurable space of results (finite, countable, or continuous). The state update is `ρ → Ω_r ρ Ω_r† / P_r` conditional on outcome r.** This is the form the Belavkin probe correctly identified — but it is DWM, not Belavkin 1989/1992.

The `M_v = ⟨v|U|0⟩` form is the **Stinespring dilation** of this Kraus map: an explicit factorization `Ω_r = ⟨r|U|0⟩` where U is the system-bath unitary and {|r⟩} is the bath measurement basis. Wiseman 1996 §2 doesn't write this verbatim (he leaves Ω_r abstract), but he derives it implicitly in §3.1's coupling to a bath; Plenio-Knight 1998 derives it explicitly (see §2 below).

## 2. Plenio-Knight 1998 — explicit `M = P_0 U P_0` Kraus operators with bath labels, derived from Davies machinery

### §IV.A "Quantum jumps", pp. 22–27 (lines 810–1020 of extracted text):

Plenio-Knight present the Hegerfeldt-Wilser / Dalibard-Castin-Mølmer derivation of the per-emission Kraus update.

Eq. (49)–(55), p. 22:

> "Rρ_A^{(n)}(t) = ∑_{i=1}^{2} 2 Γ_{ii} |0⟩⟨i| ρ_A^{(n)}(t) |i⟩⟨0|"   (55)

The structural form `R(ρ) = ∑_i V_i ρ V_i†` with `V_i = √(2Γ_{ii})|0⟩⟨i|` is the **discrete sum-over-bath-labels Kraus operator family**. The bath label i indexes the discrete emission channel.

Eq. (60), p. 24:

> "p_{[0,t]}(t_1,...,t_n) = tr_A{S(t,t_n) R ... R S(t_1, 0) ρ_A(0)}
>     = P_0(t,t_n) I_1(t_n, t_{n-1}) ... I_1(t_1, 0)."

This is the **multi-time Kraus factorization** along a trajectory of emission times — the Davies-Srinivas continuous-measurement form.

Eq. (64), p. 26, the **`M = P_0 U P_0` (no-detection) Kraus operator**:

> "|ψ(s_n)⟩ = P_0 U(s_n, s_{n-1}) P_0 ... P_0 U(s_1, 0)|ψ(0)⟩"   (64)

This is exactly the `M_0 = ⟨0|U|0⟩` Stinespring/Kraus form: P_0 = |0⟩⟨0| is the projector onto the bath vacuum (the "no detection" outcome). Composing across times produces a Kraus operator string indexed by the observation history.

### Explicit Davies attribution, p. 23 (lines 879–881):

> "Zoller, Marte and Walls then realized that the structure of these expressions coincides with that derived from an abstract theory of continuous measurement constructed by Srinivas and Davies (Davies, 1969; Davies, 1970; Davies, 1971; Davies, 1976; Srinivas and Davies, 1981; Srinivas and Davies, 1982)."

References, lines 3927–3930, 4170–4171:

> "Davies, E.B., 1969, Comm. Math. Phys. 15, 277.
> Davies, E.B., 1970, Comm. Math. Phys. 19, 83.
> Davies, E.B., 1971, Comm. Math. Phys. 22, 51.
> Davies, E.B., Quantum Theory of Open Systems (Academic, New York, 1976).
> Srinivas, M.D., and E.B. Davies, 1981, Opt. Acta 28, 981.
> Srinivas, M.D. and E.B. Davies, 1982, Opt. Acta 29, 235."

### Cardinality of outcome alphabet in Plenio-Knight:

The Plenio-Knight Kraus sum (eq. 55) is over `i = 1, 2` (2 atomic transitions) — finite. But the **trajectory** (sequence of emission times) is over a countably-infinite product space `(t_1, ..., t_n)` with arbitrary n, then summed over n in eq. (51). The full Davies-Srinivas instrument is over the **disjoint union of all n-emission-time configurations** — naturally countably-infinite cardinality on the path level.

## 3. Belavkin 1992 CMP — VERIFIED: continuous-time CCR/Fock/QSDE, NOT discrete Kraus

### §1 "Introduction", p. 612 (lines 92–123 of extracted text):

> "We do not suppose that the operators L_x are pairwise commutative or even self-adjoint or normal. But we at first assume that they are bounded, L_x ∈ L(ℋ), almost everywhere on the space R+ × Λ with respect to the product λ(dx) = dt dλ of a positive measure dλ ≡ λ(dx) on the Borel space Λ and the standard Lebesgue measure dt on R+."

The base measure is Lebesgue × λ on `R+ × Λ` — **continuous time + arbitrary outcome space Λ**.

> "The basic idea is that the quantum system under an observation must be described as the subsystem of a big system, including a Boson field A as a model of an observation channel coupled to the system by a singular interaction."

(lines 114–118.) — Bath = Boson field, not discrete bath outcomes.

### §2 "A Posteriori Quantum Dynamics", p. 615 (lines 153–168):

> "Let us consider the quantum stochastic evolution U_t, t ∈ R+ ... given by the Hudson-Parthasarathy operator equation [1, 17]
>
>   dU + KU dt = ∫_Λ (L_x dA*(t,dx) − L_x* dA(t,dx)) U,    U_0 = I,   (5)"

(lines 153–157.) — Belavkin's evolution is the **Hudson-Parthasarathy QSDE**, exactly the framework `QSC_DISPOSITION.md` rejected for Syracuse.

### Non-demolition and abelian observation, p. 614–615, Theorem 1(b):

> "The family {X(t) = j(t,X) | X ∈ ℒ} satisfies the nondemolition condition
>
>   [X(s), Y(t,E)] = 0, ∀ s ≤ t   (8)
>
> with respect to the given complex output fields Y(t,E) ∈ {B(t,E), B*(t,E)}"

(lines 213–216.)

> "B(t,E), B*(t,E) and, hence, the real nondemolition process Y(t) = B(t) + B*(t) are locally unitary equivalent to the input fields A(t), A*(t) and to the commutative process Q(t)"

(lines 231–233.) — **Y(t) is commutative** (abelian), and the system observable algebra X(t) commutes with the past observation algebra Y(s), s ≤ t. This is the non-demolition definition.

### §3 "A Posteriori State", eq. (16), p. 617:

> "π_t(X) = ⟨ψ|X(t)ψ⟩"  + propagator equations.

The Belavkin filter is the conditional expectation of the system observable given the observation field history.

### Cardinality in Belavkin 1992:

Outcome space Λ is "a Borel space" (lines 92–97) — admits both finite, countable, and continuous outcomes structurally. **But** the observation process Y(t,E) = B(t,E) + B*(t,E) is the real part of a Bose annihilation field — **continuous time with Wiener-noise statistics** (eq. for `<0|dQ(t,E')dQ(t,E)|0> = dt λ(E ∩ E')`, line 182). Discrete-time evolution + discrete Geom(1/2) outcomes is not the native object.

### Verdict on Belavkin 1992

**Belavkin 1992 IS continuous-time QSDE/HP-Fock filtering with a posteriori state derived from a Bose-field observation process. It is NOT the discrete-time Kraus form `M_v = ⟨v|U|0⟩`.** The connection to Syracuse runs through DWM (Davies-Wiseman-Milburn discrete Kraus), not Belavkin's continuous-time CCR. The Belavkin-audit's Finding 1.1 stands confirmed at verbatim level.

## 4. Davies / DWM countably-infinite-outcome admissibility — verbatim status

### Direct evidence in Wiseman 1996 §2:

> "∑_r F_r(T) = 1.     (7)"
> "The set of all effects {F(r)} constitutes a positive-operator-valued measure (povm) on the space of results r [50]."

The phrase "**a positive-operator-valued measure on the space of results r**" admits arbitrary measurable cardinality (POVMs by definition are measures on σ-algebras; finite, countable, continuous all valid). The r-sum form `∑_r` covers any cardinality once one allows it to be integration in the continuous case.

### Direct evidence in Davies-Srinivas (via Plenio-Knight citation):

The Plenio-Knight reference path (Davies 1969 / 1970 / 1971 / 1976 + Srinivas-Davies 1981/1982) is described as "**an abstract theory of continuous measurement**" — and the trajectory space is over arbitrary n-emission times (countably infinite cardinality on the path level, eq. (51), `∑_{n=0}^∞`).

### Davies's monograph (1976, Academic Press) — NOT pulled this session

The book itself is paywalled physical media. Standard texts (Davies-Lewis 1970 CMP 17:239, Davies 1976 Ch. 2 "Instruments and Effects") define an **instrument** as a σ-additive measure on the result-space σ-algebra taking values in CP maps on the state-space. This admits **any measurable cardinality** of outcomes — finite, countably infinite, continuous, or mixed.

## 5. The Stinespring dilation form `M_v = ⟨v|U|0⟩` — where verbatim found

**Wiseman 1996** does NOT write `M_v = ⟨v|U|0⟩` literally; he leaves Ω_r abstract.

**Plenio-Knight 1998** eq. (64) gives exactly this form: `|ψ(s_n)⟩ = P_0 U(s_n, s_{n-1}) P_0 ...|ψ(0)⟩` where `P_0 = |0⟩⟨0|` projects to the bath vacuum. Bath outcome "no-photon" corresponds to v=0; emission outcomes correspond to `|i⟩⟨0|` projectors (eq. 55). The Kraus operator for outcome v is then `M_v = ⟨v|U|0⟩` — the partial inner product against the bath outcome basis. Plenio-Knight write this in pieces but the explicit Stinespring projection is eq. (64).

**Standard quantum-trajectory textbook form** (Wiseman-Milburn 2010 Ch. 1–3, Nielsen-Chuang 2000 Thm. 8.1, Kraus 1983 monograph): for any CP-trace-preserving map Φ on B(ℋ_S), there exist a bath ℋ_B, a bath vacuum |0⟩, a system-bath unitary U on ℋ_S ⊗ ℋ_B, and an ONB {|v⟩}_v of ℋ_B such that

  Φ(ρ) = ∑_v M_v ρ M_v†,    M_v := ⟨v|U|0⟩ ∈ B(ℋ_S),    ∑_v M_v† M_v = I_S.

This is the **Stinespring dilation theorem** — established by Stinespring (1955), Kraus (1971/1983), Davies-Lewis (1970), and standard.

For Syracuse: ℋ_S = L²((ℤ/3^n)*, π_n), ℋ_B = ℓ²(ℕ_{≥1}, Geom(1/2)), with {|v⟩}_{v≥1} the Geom(1/2) ONB. Existence of U is guaranteed by Stinespring once T_j is verified CP — which is straightforward (T_j is the conditional expectation of a positive Tao-recursion kernel; positive on positive states).

## 6. Verdict on `M_v = ⟨v|U|0⟩` for Syracuse

The Kraus form is **DWM-canonical** — written abstractly in Wiseman 1996 §2 (eq. 5–11), implicitly in §3 (eq. 21 sum-over-r), and explicitly in Plenio-Knight 1998 eq. (64) for the photodetection case. The general Stinespring `M_v = ⟨v|U|0⟩` form is textbook-standard (Davies 1976 Ch. 2, Wiseman-Milburn 2010 Ch. 1, Kraus 1983) and applies to any CP map.

For Syracuse's T_j the Stinespring dilation exists because T_j is CP (positivity preserving, as a sum of positive kernels with positive weights). The Kraus operators take the explicit form (from `BELAVKIN_SYRACUSE_IDENTIFICATION.md` §2):

  M_v^{(j, b_{[1,j-1]})} f(ξ) = 2^{-v/2} · A_v^{(j)}(ξ, b_{[1,j-1]}) · f(ξ · 2^{-v} mod 3^n)

with `∑_{v≥1} (M_v^{(j)})† M_v^{(j)} = I_{ℋ_n}` (verified by the Geom(1/2) resolution `∑_v 2^{-v} = 1`).

**The form is literally correct under DWM.** The Belavkin probe's brand-label "Belavkin" was wrong (Belavkin 1992 is continuous-time CCR); but the operator-level identification ports cleanly to DWM.
