# Phase 3 — B-Centered Moment Calculations

**Date:** 2026-05-14  
**Depends on:** AMALG_FREENESS_SETUP.md, AMALG_FREENESS_SUBALGEBRA_CHECK.md  
**Verbatim definition (Voiculescu 1995, §1.2, page 245):**

> "The family (A_i)_{i∈I} will be called free if φ(a₁a₂ ··· aₙ) = 0 whenever aⱼ ∈ A_{iⱼ} with i₁ ≠ i₂ ≠ ··· ≠ iₙ and φ(aⱼ) = 0 for 1 ≤ j ≤ n."

---

## 1. Computing φ(T_j)

The conditional expectation φ = E_B averages T_j over the new randomness introduced at step j, holding fixed the accumulated sum b_{[1,j]}.

Recall from C1_TAO_RECURSION_FORM.md:
> T_j(f)(ξ) = Σ_{v ≥ 1} 2^{-v} · χ(3^{2j-2} · 2^{-(b_{[1,j-1]} + v)} · (2^{a_{2j}} + 3)) · f(ξ · 2^{-v} mod 3^n)

where v = v_j ~ Geom(2) is independent of all prior increments.

Conditioning on B = σ({b_{[1,k]} : k ≥ 1}) fixes all the running sums. In particular, it fixes b_{[1,j]} = b_{[1,j-1]} + v_j. Given b_{[1,j]} fixed, v_j is **determined** by b_{[1,j-1]}: v_j = b_{[1,j]} - b_{[1,j-1]}. Therefore:

> φ(T_j)(ξ) = E[T_j(f)(ξ) | b_{[1,j]}]
>           = Σ_{v ≥ 1} 2^{-v} · χ(3^{2j-2} · 2^{-b_{[1,j]}} · (2^{b_{[1,j]} - b_{[1,j-1]}} + 3)) · f(ξ · 2^{-v} mod 3^n) · 1_{v = b_{[1,j]} - b_{[1,j-1]}}

(The indicator 1_{v = b_{[1,j]} - b_{[1,j-1]}} selects the unique value of v_j consistent with the conditioned accumulator. Since b_{[1,j]} = b_{[1,j-1]} + v_j and all values are fixed, there is no remaining randomness to average over.)

**Key conclusion:** φ(T_j) is a **B-measurable operator** — it is a function of {b_{[1,k]} : k ≤ j} only, with no residual randomness. Explicitly:

> φ(T_j)(ξ) = 2^{-(b_{[1,j]} - b_{[1,j-1]})} · χ(3^{2j-2} · 2^{-b_{[1,j]}} · (2^{b_{[1,j]} - b_{[1,j-1]}} + 3)) · f(ξ · 2^{-(b_{[1,j]} - b_{[1,j-1]})} mod 3^n)

This is a **rank-1 weighted shift** (at a specific shift determined by b_{[1,j]} - b_{[1,j-1]}) composed with a phase, and is B-measurable.

---

## 2. The centered operator T̃_j := T_j - φ(T_j)

Since φ(T_j) is the conditional expectation, T̃_j = T_j - φ(T_j) captures the residual variation. However, since conditioning on B fully determines v_j (as shown above), **T̃_j = 0** pointwise: once all b_{[1,k]} are fixed, T_j itself is determined and has no residual.

**This is the first structural obstruction.** The centered operators T̃_j = T_j - φ(T_j) vanish identically given the conditioning. This makes all moment calculations of the form φ(T̃_{j₁} ··· T̃_{jₙ}) = φ(0 ··· 0) = 0 trivially.

However, this trivialization reveals a more fundamental issue: the operators T_j are not elements of A in the role of "B-free random variables" in the Voiculescu sense. Rather, the T_j are **B-measurable** — they lie in the subalgebra generated by B (up to the deterministic shift structure). The freeness question is therefore vacuously satisfied, not genuinely so.

---

## 3. Revised setup: the correct "random" part

To obtain a non-trivial B-freeness question, one must separate the T_j into their genuinely random component (the part not determined by B) and their B-measurable part. Let us re-examine the structure more carefully.

**In Tao's product form** (C1, eq. 7.5), the quantity being analyzed is the product:

> Π_{j=1}^{n/2} |f(3^{2j-2} · 2^{-b_{[1,j]}}, b_j)|

where b_j = v_{2j-1} + v_{2j} (the pair-sum at step j) and b_{[1,j]} = Σ_{k=1}^j b_k (the accumulated sum). Here f(x, b) is the conditional characteristic function at the pair (x, b).

The relevant "operator" at step j is not T_j alone but the factor F_j := |f(3^{2j-2} · 2^{-b_{[1,j]}}, b_j)|. Each F_j depends on:
1. b_{[1,j-1]} (the prior accumulated sum, B-measurable) — through the x-argument
2. b_j (the new pair-sum at step j, not B-measurable before step j is taken)

So the genuine "new information" at step j is b_j, and F_j conditional on b_{[1,j-1]} is a function of b_j alone.

## 4. Second-order B-centered moment: n=2 case

Let j₁ = 1, j₂ = 2. The B-freeness condition requires:

> φ(T̃_{j₁} · T̃_{j₂}) = 0

**Setup.** In Tao's pair form:
- F_1 depends on b_1 (= b_{[1,1]}, new at step 1) with x_1 = 3^0 · 2^{-0} = 1 (no prior accumulation)
- F_2 depends on b_2 (= b_{[1,2]} - b_{[1,1]}, new at step 2) with x_2 = 3^2 · 2^{-b_{[1,1]}} (depending on b_{[1,1]}, which is B-measurable after step 1)

**φ(F_1 - φ(F_1)).** Since F_1 depends only on b_1, and b_1 ∈ B (it is b_{[1,1]}), we have F_1 = φ(F_1). So T̃_1 = F_1 - φ(F_1) = 0 given b_1 ∈ B.

**The same holds for F_2:** F_2 depends on (b_{[1,1]}, b_2) where b_2 is the new increment; both b_{[1,1]} and b_2 = b_{[1,2]} - b_{[1,1]} are B-measurable. So F_2 = φ(F_2).

**Conclusion for n = 2 second-order moment:**

> φ(T̃_1 · T̃_2) = φ(0 · 0) = 0 ✓ (trivially)

But this is trivial vanishing, not structural freeness.

## 7. Re-examination: the off-diagonal correction as the genuine "free" part

The triviality above arises because b_{[1,j]} fully determines T_j via conditioning. The genuinely non-trivial operators are the **off-diagonal corrections Off_j** from result_77_T_lead_spectrum.md:

> (P_+, P_-)_{n+1} = T_diag · (P_+, P_-)_n + Off_n

where Off_n contains cross-frequency bilinear terms Σ_{v ≠ v'} A_v A_{v'}* that do NOT factor through b_{[1,j]}.

Let us denote X_n := Off_n, the off-diagonal correction at level n. This X_n is **not** B-measurable: it involves cross-correlations A_v(ξ) · A_{v'}*(ξ') for v ≠ v', which depend on the joint distribution of (v, v') not captured by b_{[1,j]} = v_{2j-1} + v_{2j}.

**This is the correct object to test B-freeness on.**

---

## 8. Second-order moment for off-diagonal corrections

For j₁ ≠ j₂, consider X̃_{j_k} = X_{j_k} - φ(X_{j_k}) where X_j = Off_j.

**φ(X_j).** From result_77_T_lead_spectrum.md, Off_n involves cross-frequency bilinear sums. The B-conditional expectation averages over all (v, v') with v ≠ v' subject to b_j = v + v' (the pair sum at step j). Specifically:

> φ(Off_j) = Σ_{b ≥ 2} P(b_j = b) · Σ_{v + v' = b, v ≠ v'} 2^{-v-v'} A_v A_{v'}* / P(b_j = b)
>           = Σ_{b ≥ 2} Σ_{v + v' = b, v ≠ v'} 2^{-v-v'} A_v A_{v'}*

This is non-zero in general: φ(Off_j) ≠ 0 because the off-diagonal cross-terms do not vanish under B-conditioning.

**φ(X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂}) for j₁ ≠ j₂.** Since b_{j₁} and b_{j₂} are **independent** random variables (the pair sums at distinct steps j₁, j₂ are iid Pascal(2,1/2)), we have:

> φ(X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂}) = φ((Off_{j₁} - φ(Off_{j₁})) · (Off_{j₂} - φ(Off_{j₂})))

For B-freeness this should vanish. Applying the conditional expectation:

> φ(X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂}) = E_B[X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂}]

Since Off_{j₁} and Off_{j₂} depend on distinct, **independent** pair-groups (j₁ and j₂ respectively), and conditioned on B the pair sums b_{j₁} and b_{j₂} are fixed, the remaining variation in X_{j₁} | b_{j₁} and X_{j₂} | b_{j₂} are independent. Therefore:

> E_B[X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂}] = E_B[X̃_{j₁}] · E_B[X̃_{j₂}] = 0 · 0 = 0 ✓

where the last step uses E_B[X̃_{j_k}] = E_B[X_{j_k} - E_B(X_{j_k})] = 0 by definition of centering.

**This vanishing is non-trivial and structural.** It holds because the off-diagonal corrections at distinct steps j₁ ≠ j₂ depend on independent pair-groups.

## 9. Third-order alternating: φ(X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂} · X̃_{j₁})

This is the decisive test. Write:

> φ(X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂} · X̃_{j₁}) = E_B[(Off_{j₁} - φ(Off_{j₁})) · (Off_{j₂} - φ(Off_{j₂})) · (Off_{j₁} - φ(Off_{j₁}))]

The operator Off_{j₁} appears **twice** (at positions 1 and 3), sandwiching Off_{j₂}.

**Structural question:** does the product Off_{j₁} · Off_{j₂} · Off_{j₁} decouple given B?

Off_{j₁} depends on the pair (v_{2j₁-1}, v_{2j₁}) with b_{j₁} = v_{2j₁-1} + v_{2j₁} fixed by B.  
Off_{j₂} depends on the pair (v_{2j₂-1}, v_{2j₂}) with b_{j₂} = v_{2j₂-1} + v_{2j₂} fixed by B.

The **phase argument of Off_{j₂}** is (from C1, eq. 7.4):

> x_{j₂} = 3^{2j₂-2} · 2^{-b_{[1,j₂]}}

where b_{[1,j₂]} = b_1 + b_2 + ··· + b_{j₂} depends on b_{j₁} when j₁ < j₂ (since b_{j₁} is one of the summands). Thus:

> x_{j₂} = 3^{2j₂-2} · 2^{-(b_1 + ··· + b_{j₁} + ··· + b_{j₂})}

**The phase argument x_{j₂} depends on b_{j₁}**, which is B-measurable. Therefore Off_{j₂} has a phase factor that is a function of b_{j₁} — meaning Off_{j₁} and Off_{j₂} are **correlated through the B-measurable factor b_{j₁} in the phase of Off_{j₂}**.

Explicitly, conditioning on B fixes b_{j₁} and b_{j₂}. With b_{j₁} fixed:
- Off_{j₁} | b_{j₁} is a function of (v_{2j₁-1}, v_{2j₁}) with v_{2j₁-1} + v_{2j₁} = b_{j₁} fixed
- Off_{j₂} | (b_{j₁}, b_{j₂}) depends on (v_{2j₂-1}, v_{2j₂}) with v_{2j₂-1} + v_{2j₂} = b_{j₂} fixed, AND has a phase depending on b_{j₁} through x_{j₂} = 3^{2j₂-2} · 2^{-b_{[1,j₂]}}

The phase dependence of Off_{j₂} on b_{j₁} means that Off_{j₂} is not independent of the B-measurable content associated with step j₁. **However**, crucially, conditioning on B already fixes b_{j₁} (it is B-measurable). Therefore, given B:

> Off_{j₂} | B = function of (v_{2j₂-1}, v_{2j₂}) only, with b_{j₁} entering as a fixed parameter

This means Off_{j₂} | B and Off_{j₁} | B are **conditionally independent** (their remaining randomness — the individual (v, v') pairs — are from independent pair-groups). The phase parameter b_{j₁} in Off_{j₂} is fixed by B, so it acts as a B-measurable scalar.

**Computing φ(X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂} · X̃_{j₁}):**

> = E_B[X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂} · X̃_{j₁}]

Let Z_k = X_{j_k} | B − E_B(X_{j_k}) be the centered conditionally-independent components. Since Z_1 and Z_2 are conditionally independent given B:

> E_B[Z_1 · Z_2 · Z_1] = E_B[Z_1² · Z_2]  (if Z_1, Z_2 commuted — they do not in general)

In the non-commutative setting this does not factor as E_B[Z_1²] · E_B[Z_2]. However, the **key issue** is whether the phase correlation between Off_{j₂} and the indexing at step j₁ creates a non-zero contribution.

**Explicit calculation for n = 3 (j₁ = 1, j₂ = 2):**

Write Off_1 = Σ_{v ≠ v', v+v'=b_1} c_{v,v'} P_{v,v'} where P_{v,v'} is the cross-frequency projection.

Write Off_2 = Σ_{v ≠ v', v+v'=b_2} c_{v,v'} · e^{i θ(b_1)} · P_{v,v'} where the phase e^{i θ(b_1)} = e^{-2πi ξ · 3^2 · 2^{-b_{[1,2]}}} depends on b_1 = b_{[1,1]}.

Given B (fixing b_1, b_2):
- X̃_1 = Off_1 - E_B(Off_1) = Σ_{v ≠ v', v+v'=b_1} c_{v,v'} (P_{v,v'} - E[P_{v,v'}|b_1])
- X̃_2 = Off_2 - E_B(Off_2) = e^{i θ(b_1)} · Σ_{v ≠ v', v+v'=b_2} c_{v,v'} (P_{v,v'} - E[P_{v,v'}|b_2])

Note that e^{i θ(b_1)} is B-measurable (b_1 is fixed by B). Therefore:

> X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂} · X̃_{j₁}
> = X̃_1 · (e^{i θ(b_1)} · Ŷ_2) · X̃_1
> = e^{i θ(b_1)} · X̃_1 · Ŷ_2 · X̃_1    (since e^{i θ(b_1)} ∈ B commutes through to front)

where Ŷ_2 = Σ_{v ≠ v', v+v'=b_2} c_{v,v'} (P_{v,v'} - E[P_{v,v'}|b_2]) is the centered off-diagonal at step 2, stripped of the B-measurable phase.

Taking φ = E_B:

> E_B[e^{i θ(b_1)} · X̃_1 · Ŷ_2 · X̃_1] = e^{i θ(b_1)} · E_B[X̃_1 · Ŷ_2 · X̃_1]

The remaining factor E_B[X̃_1 · Ŷ_2 · X̃_1] involves X̃_1 (function of (v_1, v_2) | b_1 fixed) and Ŷ_2 (function of (v_3, v_4) | b_2 fixed). These are **conditionally independent** given B.

For conditionally independent centered variables Z_1, Z_2 in the non-commutative sense, the product E_B[Z_1 · Z_2 · Z_1] need not vanish. In the **scalar** (B = C) free probability setting, this moment vanishes by the free cumulant characterization if and only if Z_1 and Z_2 are free. In the **operator-valued** (B non-trivial) setting, conditional independence does NOT imply B-freeness.

**Explicit non-vanishing.** Let Z_1 = P_{v_1, v_1'} (cross-frequency projection at step 1) and Z_2 = P_{v_2, v_2'} (at step 2). Both are centered: E[Z_k] = 0 by construction. Then:

> E[Z_1 · Z_2 · Z_1] = E[P_{v_1, v_1'} · P_{v_2, v_2'} · P_{v_1, v_1'}]

In the bilinear form M_n(η) = Σ_ξ μ̂_n(ξ) μ̂_n*(ξ η), the cross-frequency projections P_{v,v'} contribute terms of the form μ̂_n(ξ · 2^{-v}) μ̂_n*(ξ · 2^{-v'}). The composition P_{v_1,v_1'} · P_{v_2,v_2'} · P_{v_1,v_1'} involves:

> Σ_ξ Σ_ξ' Σ_ξ'' μ̂(ξ) μ̂*(ξ · 2^{-v_1+v_1'}) · μ̂(ξ') μ̂*(ξ' · 2^{-v_2+v_2'}) · μ̂(ξ'') μ̂*(ξ'' · 2^{-v_1+v_1'})

Subject to the trace structure and Plancherel, this triple product does **not** vanish in general. From result_76_conservation_law.md, the bilinear moments M_n(η) for η = 1+3^{n-1} take value S_{n+1}/(-2) ≠ 0. The triple-product analog involves M_n^{(3)}(η_1, η_2), a third-order bilinear moment, which is not constrained to vanish by the conservation law (Theorem 76.1 applies to Σ_j M_{n+1}(η_0 + j·3^n) = 0, a second-order moment identity with no third-order analog).

**The third-order moment φ(X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂} · X̃_{j₁}) does not vanish generically.**

## 11. The decisive computation

The moment φ(X̃_{j₁} · X̃_{j₂} · X̃_{j₁}) is decisive. It corresponds (in the bilinear pair-form language of result_76_conservation_law.md) to a **third-order mixed moment**:

> Σ_ξ μ̂_{n,j₁}(ξ) · μ̂_{n,j₂}(ξ · η) · μ̂_{n,j₁}*(ξ · η · η')

For specific η, η' arising from the (v, v') cross-frequency couplings at steps j₁ and j₂. This is not constrained to zero by:
- The conservation law Theorem 76.1 (which gives Σ_j M_{n+1}(η + j·3^n) = 0, a second-order constraint)
- The leading-mode identity Theorem 76.3 (second-order)
- The T_diag eigenstructure (which acts on P_± not on the triple correlation)

The numerical value of this third-order moment can be estimated from the empirical data in result_77_T_lead_spectrum.md: the off-diagonal correction ratio at k=5→6 is +0.503, indicating the off-diagonal terms are non-negligible. The third-order product of off-diagonal terms with the same-step operator appearing twice will inherit a contribution of order (Off_{j₁})² · Off_{j₂} ~ (1/2)^{j₁} · (1/2)^{j₂}, which is non-zero.
