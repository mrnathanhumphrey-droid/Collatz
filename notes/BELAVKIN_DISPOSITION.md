## 2. The decisive structural feature

The decisive feature distinguishing Belavkin from all prior probes:

**Belavkin's operators-at-time-$j$ are unconstrained by any *-homomorphism / time-translation structure.** Each step's Kraus operator $M_v^{(j)} = \langle v | U_j | 0\rangle$ is determined by an INDEPENDENT unitary $U_j$ — which may further depend adaptively on past observations.

Compare:
- **HP/AP/QSC:** The Itô calculus presumes "stationary noise" — each time-slice has the same Fock-space structure, with the Itô differentials $(dA, dA^\dagger, d\Lambda)$ playing the same algebraic role at each time. Level-graded operators that depend non-trivially on $j$ are NOT a natural object.
- **AFL:** The *-homomorphism embeddings $j_t : \mathcal O \to \mathcal A$ transport a FIXED algebra $\mathcal O$ to time $t$. The "random variable at time $t$" is the same random variable, just at a different time-position. Level-graded operators that are NOT time-translates of a fixed $\mathcal O$ are NOT admitted by AFL's axioms.
- **Belavkin:** Each $U_j$ is an independent (possibly time-dependent, possibly observation-history-adaptive) unitary, and the resulting Kraus operators $M_v^{(j, b_{[1, j-1]})}$ at different $j$ are NOT related by any algebraic structure. Level-graded adaptive Kraus operators are the **canonical setup**, not an extension.

This is what makes Belavkin the natural home for Syracuse: the Tao recursion's $T_j$ has phase factor $\chi_j(b_{[1, j-1]})$ that depends on the step $j$ AND on the cumulative past — exactly the canonical Belavkin adaptive Kraus structure.

## 3. Why row (d) is non-zero in Belavkin

The key calculation, summarized from `BELAVKIN_MOMENT_PREDICTIONS.md` §3:

In AFL with abelian filtration + kernel condition $\mathcal P(a_*) = 0$:
$$ \varphi(j_{j_1}(a) j_{j_2}(b) j_{j_1}(c)) = \varphi_0(a c \cdot \mathcal P^{j_2 - j_1}(b)) = \varphi_0(a c \cdot 0) = 0 . $$

In Belavkin with level-graded adaptive Kraus $M^{(j, b_{[1, j-1]})}$:
$$ \varphi(\tilde X_{j_1} \tilde X_{j_2} \tilde X_{j_1}) = \mathbb E_{(v_{1:j_2})}\left[ \mathrm{Tr}\left( \tilde X_{j_1}^{\text{op}} \cdot M^{(j_2)} \cdot \tilde X_{j_1}^{\text{op}} \cdots \right) \right] . $$

In the AFL formula, the third $j_{j_1}(c)$ factor commutes with $j_{j_2}(b)$ via abelian filtration, merging $j_{j_1}(a) j_{j_1}(c) \to j_{j_1}(a c)$, and then the kernel condition $\mathcal P(b) = 0$ kills the residual. **There is no analog of this commutativity-and-contraction step in Belavkin.**

In Belavkin, the two appearances of $\tilde X_{j_1}^{\text{op}}$ at positions surrounding the step-$j_2$ Kraus operator do NOT commute past it (the Kraus operator at $j_2$ is an arbitrary operator on $\mathcal H_S$, not a *-homomorphism image of anything). The product $\tilde X_{j_1}^{\text{op}} \cdot M^{(j_2)} \cdot \tilde X_{j_1}^{\text{op}}$ is a non-trivial operator on $\mathcal H_S$, and its trace against the trajectory state is generically non-zero.

**This is why Belavkin succeeds where AFL failed.**
