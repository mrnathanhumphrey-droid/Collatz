## 2. Bi-monotone independence — fit check

**Definition (recap, GHS 2017 Defn 2.1 + 2.4):** linearly ordered family of pairs of algebras (A_{k,ℓ}, A_{k,r})_{k ∈ K}, joint distribution defined via c-bi-free realization on the unitized two-faced free product.

### 2.1 Threshold structural issue

Bi-monotone requires a **two-faced** structure: each algebra in the family is a PAIR (A_{k,ℓ}, A_{k,r}). Syracuse has a SINGLE algebra A_j = B_marginal⟨X̃_j⟩_0 per step j, generated by a SINGLE operator X̃_j. There is no natural "left/right" partition of A_j into (A_{j,ℓ}, A_{j,r}).

**Workaround attempt:** trivialize the right algebra (set A_{k,r} = C · 1 for all k, or duplicate A_k as A_{k,ℓ} = A_{k,r} = A_k). Under both workarounds, bi-monotone Defn 2.1 collapses to standard monotone (since c-bi-free with trivial right factor reduces to c-free with trivial second state, which by Franz [7] is monotone — see GHS 2017 p4 "The monotonic product can also be realized as a c-free product").

So **under any natural Syracuse-compatible reduction, bi-monotone degenerates to monotone**. Row (f) then fails by the same H1' mechanism.

## 3. Indented / α-free product — fit check

**Definition (recap, Hasebe 2010 Defn α-free / equation (1.12)):** linearly ordered family of triplets (A_λ, φ_λ, ψ_λ, θ_λ)_{λ ∈ Λ}; α-free product produces a joint distribution on the free product algebra parameterized by all three states.

### 3.1 Threshold structural issue: 3 states required

Syracuse has a SINGLE conditional expectation ϕ = E_{B_∞}. To use α-free, one must supply two additional states (ψ, θ) on each A_j. Three natural choices:

(N1) **ψ = θ = ϕ.** Under this triplet, the α-free product specializes to which case? Hasebe 2010 Section 1.4 lists the specializations:
   - (ψ_1, θ_1) = (ψ_1, ψ_1) and (ψ_2, θ_2) = (ψ_2, ψ_2) recovers c-monotone restricted to ψ_1 = θ_1.
   - With all three equal, the α-free product collapses further to either monotone, Boolean, or free depending on which sub-relation is being invoked.

(N2) **ψ = 0 (delta state), θ = ϕ.** Specializes (via Hasebe 2010 p7 eqn (1.9) and Franz [13]) to the monotone product. Same failure as monotone.

(N3) **Choose ψ to be a non-trivial second state on A_j giving non-zero diagonal moments.** No natural second state on A_j arises from Syracuse — the construction supplies only ϕ = E_{B_∞}.

Under all three natural choices, α-free **specializes to monotone, anti-monotone, c-monotone, free, or Boolean** depending on the triplet selection (Hasebe 2010 p7).

### 3.2 Direct moment-pattern check (best attempt at non-degenerate specialization)

The closest non-degenerate sub-case is **c-monotone** (ψ ≠ θ, both states distinct on A_j). c-monotone uses BOTH states ϕ and ψ in the peak-rule substitution:

Hasebe 2010 Defn 1.12 (CM2):
> φ(a_1 · · · a_n) = φ(a_1 · · · a_{p−1}) · [φ(a_p) − ψ(a_p)] · φ(a_{p+1} · · · a_n) + ψ(a_p) · φ(a_1 · · · a_{p−1} a_{p+1} · · · a_n)

This is a TWO-TERM peak rule (the standard monotone-product peak rule is a special case when ψ(a_p) = φ(a_p)).

For 4-alternating ϕ(X̃_{j_1} X̃_{j_2} X̃_{j_1} X̃_{j_2}) with j_1 < j_2, peaks at i=2 and i=4:

At peak i=2 (interior):
   ϕ(X̃_{j_1} X̃_{j_2} X̃_{j_1} X̃_{j_2})
   = ϕ(X̃_{j_1}) [ϕ(X̃_{j_2}) − ψ(X̃_{j_2})] ϕ(X̃_{j_1} X̃_{j_2}) + ψ(X̃_{j_2}) ϕ(X̃_{j_1} X̃_{j_1} X̃_{j_2})

Under centering ϕ(X̃_{j_1}) = ϕ(X̃_{j_2}) = 0: first term vanishes. Second term: ψ(X̃_{j_2}) · ϕ(X̃_{j_1}² X̃_{j_2}). Then ϕ(X̃_{j_1}² X̃_{j_2}): peak at i=3 endpoint, j_2 > j_1, substitute X̃_{j_2} → 0 → second term also vanishes.

**Predicted: 0 under c-monotone with marginal centering and any choice of ψ centered as well.** Syracuse value 6.09×10⁻¹ ≠ 0. ✗ FAIL.

Could a NON-centered ψ rescue it? Hasebe 2010 (1.5)-(1.7) example computation indicates that even with non-centered ψ, the peak rule at j_2 forces ϕ(X̃_{j_2}) and ψ(X̃_{j_2}) to control the moment. Syracuse construction provides only one natural state ϕ — there is no natural ψ that differs from ϕ on A_j.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a)-(e) | … | OK after centering | matches | ✓ |
| **(f)** | 4-alternating | 0 under c-monotone two-term peak rule with any centered ψ. Non-zero only if ψ exists that violates centering, but no such ψ is natural for Syracuse. | non-zero | ✗ FAIL |
| (g) | asymmetry | α-free preserves the linear ordering — OK in direction | yes | ✓ direction |

**Verdict: indented/α-free FAILS row (f).** Three failure modes:
- (i) Syracuse provides only 1 state, not 3; natural specializations all reduce to monotone or c-monotone, both of which fail (f).
- (ii) Even with non-centered ψ as a free parameter, the two-term peak rule still gives 0 under centering of both states.
- (iii) iid-copies requirement (Hasebe 2011 Defn 8.2) is identical to monotone.

## 4. Cross-cutting structural failure mode

All three Tier 1 candidates fail at row (f) (4-alternating non-vanishing). The mechanism is shared:

**Common failure mechanism.** Each Tier 1 candidate's peak rule (or generalization thereof) treats the algebra element X_i at peak position i as a single algebra element with a well-defined moment under ϕ (or under one of the states). The substitution X_i → ϕ(X_i) is structurally valid ONLY IF a separate instance of the same algebra A_λ_i appearing elsewhere in the word would have INDEPENDENT randomness from X_i. This independence is supplied by HS 2014 Defn 2.3's iid-copies architecture.

When the algebra A_λ contributes ONE FIXED random operator X̃_λ to the word — not multiple iid copies — the substitution at one position does NOT preserve the moment, because the residual instance(s) at other positions share randomness with X̃_λ. This is structurally row (j): Syracuse has a single X̃_j per step, NOT iid copies.

**This is order-independent** (so flipping to anti-monotone doesn't help), **two-face-independent** (so adding a right algebra doesn't help — the same iid-copies issue applies to the pair), and **state-count-independent** (so the c-monotone two-term peak rule doesn't help because both substitutions still rely on iid copies for the substituted moment to factor).
