## Asymptote fit chi²/df(k) = A − B·exp(−C·k)

**Fit on k = 40..70 alone DEGENERATES.** The window doesn't extend past peak growth, so curve_fit pushes A → ∞ to fit linear-looking data: A = 2.86e6, B ≈ A, C = 1e-5 (essentially A·C·k = linear). Numbers below are an artifact of the under-constrained fit, not a real asymptote.

### Refit using k = 40..70 + k = 80, 100 (combining with prior check probe)

With cpdfs at k=80 (1072.1) and k=100 (1147.5) added:

- **A ≈ 2158**, B ≈ 3863, C ≈ 0.0142

But residuals are large (RMS ~75, max +155 at k=80), and the model is **misspecified**. Per-step rate decays super-exponentially: 1.166 (k=40→45) → 1.075 (k=55→60) → 1.052 (k=65→70) → 1.003 (k=80→100). A single-exp model can't track that.

**Honest reading of the asymptote:** somewhere between **~1150** (taking k=100 as the plateau, given per-step rate ≈ 1.003) and **~2200** (curve_fit's central estimate). The interval is wide because of survival bias — by k=100 only 38% of trajectories are alive, and the surviving subset is enriched for "long-lived" residue patterns. A proper clean-frame asymptote would need either larger N, or a different normalization that doesn't dilute as trajectories collapse.
