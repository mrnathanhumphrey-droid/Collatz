## Verbatim setup (§4.B, p. 32)

> "The affine group over the field 𝔉 is AFF(𝔉) = {u ↦ au + b : a ∈ 𝔉*, b ∈ 𝔉} ≅ {(a b; 0 1) : a, b ∈ 𝔉, a ≠ 0}, the group generated by the canonical actions on the field 𝔉 of its additive and multiplicative groups."

> "The action of AFF(𝔉) naturally extends from the field 𝔉 to T … In this way we have realized AFF(𝔉) as a closed subgroup of AFF(T) which acts transitively on T."

> "There is a well known construction of a canonical action of the group GL(n, 𝔉) on the corresponding Bruhat-Tits building. In the case n = 2 this building is the tree T_q whose space of ends can be identified with the projective line of 𝔉. When restricted to the affine group AFF(𝔉) ⊂ GL(2, 𝔉), this action coincides with the action of AFF(𝔉) on the tree T_q [Ch]."

## Verbatim Theorem 2 (p. 17)

> "THEOREM 2.
> (a) If m_1(Φ(μ)) < ∞ and Φ(μ) < 0, then R_n o → ω almost surely.
> (b) If m_1(μ) < ∞ and Φ(μ) > 0 then (R_n o) converges almost surely to a random element of ∂*T.
> (c) If m_1(μ) < ∞, Φ(μ) = 0 and E(|o ∧ X_1 o| q^{|o∧X_1 o|}) < ∞ then R_n o → ω almost surely. This holds, in particular, under the simpler condition E(q^{|X_1|}) = ∫_Γ q^{|γ|} μ(dγ) < ∞."

## Verbatim Theorem 9 (Poisson boundary, p. 28)

> "THEOREM 9. Let μ be a probability measure on a closed subgroup Γ of AFF(T) which is irreducible, spread out and has finite first moment m_1(μ) in the group AFF(T).
> (a) If Φ(μ) ≤ 0, then the Poisson boundary of the pair (Γ, μ) is trivial.
> (b) If Φ(μ) > 0, then the Poisson boundary is the space (∂T*, ν), where ν is the distribution of R_∞."

## Disposition for E

**NO_FIT (with structural near-miss).**

The Tao-generated subgroup of AFF(ℚ_3) is *exceptional* in CKW's terminology (fixes ω ∈ ∂T_3, hence unimodular by their Theorem 1). All of CKW's main theorems (2-9) assume *non-exceptional* Γ, so they do not apply to this setting.

This is the **same observation** as BT_DISPOSITION's Negative-case Q3 conclusion: "all M_v sit in B [Borel], the parabolic-like subgroup of PGL_2(ℚ_3) stabilizing ∞". The CKW probe surfaces the same structural fact, with the additional precision: the Borel subgroup B is the *exceptional case* in CKW's classification, which is *exactly* the case CKW's main theorems exclude.

**What CKW does say about exceptional case (p. 13, Theorem 1 proof):** if Γ fixes a point on ∂T (which Tao's group does — fixes ∞), then Γ is unimodular and CKW's geometric/probabilistic toolkit doesn't apply. The harmonic measure structure doesn't exist in the way Theorem 3 describes (which requires "supp μ generates Γ as a closed semigroup"; Tao's generators all fix ∞, so the semigroup remains in the parabolic subgroup, which is exceptional).

**Mode H circular fingerprint:** None — CKW's hypotheses simply fail.

**Adelic factorization tag:** **NON_ARCH_ONLY** — CKW's framework operates entirely at a single non-archimedean place (ℚ_p for one fixed p). It does not produce an archimedean factor F_∞. So even if h2 had been satisfied, CKW would close only F_3, not F_∞ — exactly the load-bearing gap that BT_DISPOSITION identifies.

## Key structural observation

CKW Theorem 9's conclusion is that the Poisson boundary is (∂T*, ν). Tao's group fixes ω = ∞, so the analogous "boundary" for Tao would be ∂T_3 \ {∞} = 𝔓¹(ℚ_3) \ {∞} = ℚ_3. The Tao trajectory, viewed on ℚ_3, mixes uniformly over the residue classes (per BT_CANDIDATE_CONSTRUCTIONS Candidate B verbatim: v_3(r_n − 1) bounces in {0..4}, r_n mod 9 is essentially uniform). This is consistent with the *unimodular case* of CKW (random walk on the horocyclic group HOR(T) is symmetric and mixes uniformly along horocycles) — but unimodular = no nontrivial Poisson boundary structure.

So CKW's *negative* observation (exceptional / unimodular case has trivial Poisson boundary) is exactly what BT empirically found for Tao on T_3. This is consistent but doesn't help with closure.
