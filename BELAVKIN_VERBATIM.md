## 1. Belavkin's framework — structural definitions (from multi-source synthesis)

### 1.1 The two-algebra setup

The defining feature of Belavkin's quantum filtering is the **bipartition of the ambient operator algebra**:

> **System algebra** $\mathcal A_t$ (non-commutative, von Neumann algebra of system observables at time $t$)
> **Observation algebra** $\mathcal B_t$ (abelian, von Neumann algebra of the classical observation record up to time $t$)
> **Filtration** $\mathcal B_t$ is non-decreasing in $t$ (classical filtration on the observation trajectory).

The two algebras are coupled by the **non-demolition condition**:

> **Non-demolition condition.** "[X(s), Y(t)] = 0 for all s ≥ t", i.e., all future system observables must commute with the past observations.
> [Source: Wikipedia "Belavkin equation", verbatim]

This is the necessary and sufficient condition for the conditional expectation
$$ \pi_t(X) := \mathbb E[X | \mathcal B_t] $$
to be well-defined on $\mathcal A_t$ for all $X \in \mathcal A$ with values in $\mathcal B_t$.

[Source: search-result snippet, paraphrased from Belavkin 1989/1994: "A process Y(t) is called nondemolition with respect to X(t) if they commute for all t ≤ s, and this nondemolition condition together with the self-nondemolition of Y is necessary and sufficient for the existence of conditional expectations of X(t) with respect to σ-algebras generated by the family {Y(s) | s ≤ t}."]

### 1.2 Belavkin's continuous-time filtering equation (canonical form)

**Linear (unnormalized) Belavkin equation:**
$$ d\psi_t = -\left(\tfrac{1}{2} L^* L + \tfrac{i}{\hbar} H\right) \psi_t \, dt + L \psi_t \, dy_t $$
[Source: Wikipedia "Belavkin equation", verbatim]

where:
- $\psi_t$ is the unnormalized conditional state vector
- $H$ is the system Hamiltonian
- $L$ is the coupling operator (Lindblad operator)
- $y_t$ is the classical observation process (homodyne / continuous)

**Nonlinear (normalized) Belavkin posterior equation:**
$$ d\phi_t = -\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde L^* \tilde L + \tfrac{i}{\hbar} \tilde H\right) \phi_t \, dt + \tilde L \phi_t \, d\tilde w_t $$
[Source: Wikipedia "Belavkin equation", verbatim. Here $\phi_t = \psi_t / \|\psi_t\|$ and tilde-quantities are state-dependent.]

**Counting-observation Belavkin equation (Poisson):**
$$ d\psi = -\left(\tfrac{\nu}{2}(C^*C - I) + \tfrac{i}{\hbar} E\right) \psi \, dt + (C - I) \psi \, dn $$
[Source: Wikipedia "Belavkin equation", verbatim. $n(t)$ Poisson counting process; $C$ collapse / jump operator.]

**Density-operator form (linear, diffusive):**
$$ d\varrho = -[K \varrho + \varrho K^* - L \varrho L^*] dt + [L\varrho + \varrho L^*] dw $$
[Source: Wikipedia "Belavkin equation", verbatim. $K = \tfrac{1}{2} L^*L + \tfrac{i}{\hbar} H$.]

**Density-operator form (nonlinear / normalized):**
$$ d\rho = -[K\rho + \rho K^* - L \rho L^*] dt + [L\rho + \rho L^* - \rho \, \mathrm{Tr}\{(L + L^*)\rho\}] d\tilde w $$
[Source: Wikipedia, verbatim.]

### 1.3 Discrete-time form (Bouten-van Handel-James "discrete invitation" structure)

The discrete-time analog — the form most relevant to Syracuse's per-step Tao recursion — follows the standard quantum-trajectory construction (Bouten-van Handel-James 2009, "A discrete invitation"; structure summarized from search results + standard exposition):

**Setup.** A system Hilbert space $\mathcal H_S$ (finite-dimensional in the BvHJ "discrete invitation"). A bath / observation Hilbert space $\mathcal H_B$ with orthonormal basis $\{|i\rangle\}_{i \in I}$. Each time-step is a unitary interaction $U \in \mathcal U(\mathcal H_S \otimes \mathcal H_B)$ applied to $\rho \otimes |0\rangle\langle 0|$, followed by projective measurement of the bath in the basis $\{|i\rangle\}$.

**Kraus operators.** For each observation outcome $i \in I$,
$$ M_i := \langle i | \, U \, | 0 \rangle \in \mathcal B(\mathcal H_S). $$
These satisfy $\sum_i M_i^* M_i = I_{\mathcal H_S}$ (resolution of identity).

**Joint distribution of observation sequence.** For an observed trajectory $(i_1, i_2, \ldots, i_n)$,
$$ \mathbb P(i_1, \ldots, i_n) = \mathrm{Tr}\left( M_{i_n} \cdots M_{i_1} \rho_0 M_{i_1}^* \cdots M_{i_n}^* \right). $$

**Un-normalized conditional state (information state):**
$$ \sigma_n(i_1, \ldots, i_n) := M_{i_n} \cdots M_{i_1} \rho_0 M_{i_1}^* \cdots M_{i_n}^* . $$
Recursion: $\sigma_{n+1} = M_{i_{n+1}} \sigma_n M_{i_{n+1}}^*$.

**Normalized filtered state (conditional state given observation):**
$$ \pi_n(i_1, \ldots, i_n) := \frac{\sigma_n(i_1, \ldots, i_n)}{\mathrm{Tr}\, \sigma_n(i_1, \ldots, i_n)}. $$

**Multi-time expectation along trajectory.** For a system observable $A \in \mathcal B(\mathcal H_S)$ at time $n$,
$$ \mathbb E\left[ A \cdot \mathbf 1_{\{(i_1, \ldots, i_n)\}} \right] = \mathrm{Tr}\left( A \cdot \sigma_n(i_1, \ldots, i_n) \right). $$

**Conditional expectation onto observation algebra.**
$$ \pi_t(A) = \mathbb E[A | \mathcal B_t] = \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(A \sigma_t)}{\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma_t)}. $$
This is the **Belavkin filter** in the discrete setting.

[Mode-E note: the specific equation numbers from BvHJ 2009 Section 3 (Kraus operators) and Section 4 (filtering recursion) are not extracted in this pass — PDF binary read denied. The structure above is the standard expository form, consistent with the search-result excerpt "discretized atom-field interaction models, filtering equations for photon counting and homodyne detection" + standard Davies/Holevo/Bouten-van Handel-James quantum-trajectory literature.]

### 1.4 The level-graded / time-dependent Kraus form

A critical structural feature for Syracuse: the Kraus operators may be **time-dependent / level-graded**, $M_i^{(j)}$ at step $j$, if the unitary interaction at step $j$ depends on time:
$$ M_i^{(j)} := \langle i | \, U_j \, | 0 \rangle, \qquad U_j \in \mathcal U(\mathcal H_S \otimes \mathcal H_B). $$
Then
$$ \sigma_n = M_{i_n}^{(n)} \cdots M_{i_1}^{(1)} \rho_0 (M_{i_1}^{(1)})^* \cdots (M_{i_n}^{(n)})^* . $$

This is consistent with Belavkin's general framework — the unitary cocycle $U_t$ in continuous time is **not required** to be a time-translate of a fixed object. In Belavkin's notation, $U_t$ is a quantum stochastic process satisfying a Hudson-Parthasarathy cocycle equation with possibly time-dependent coefficients $L_t, H_t$.

### 1.5 Adaptive / feedback Kraus form (most general Belavkin)

In the full Belavkin feedback-control setting, the Kraus operators may depend **adaptively on the past observation history**:
$$ M_i^{(j, i_{1:j-1})} := \langle i | \, U_j(i_{1:j-1}) \, | 0 \rangle, $$
where $U_j(i_{1:j-1})$ is the unitary at step $j$ chosen as a function of the prior observations $i_1, \ldots, i_{j-1}$.

This is **the exact structural feature Syracuse needs**: the operator at step $j$ depends on the cumulative observation history $b_{[1, j-1]} = v_1 + \ldots + v_{j-1}$.

[Source: this adaptive form is the canonical feedback-Belavkin setting; for verbatim BvHJ 2009 treatment of feedback-dependent Kraus operators, see their Section 5 or 6, Mode-E gap.]

## 2. Belavkin's expectation formula for system observables along trajectory

The **multi-time joint expectation** of system observables $A_1, \ldots, A_n$ at steps $j_1 < j_2 < \ldots < j_n$, with the observation trajectory averaged out, is:

$$ \mathbb E[A_1^{(j_1)} \cdot A_2^{(j_2)} \cdots A_n^{(j_n)}] = \sum_{i_{1:N}} \mathrm{Tr}\left( A_n \cdot M_{i_N}^{(N)} \cdots A_2 \cdot M_{i_{j_2}}^{(j_2)} \cdots A_1 \cdot M_{i_{j_1}}^{(j_1)} \cdots \rho_0 \cdots \right) $$

where the trace is over the system Hilbert space $\mathcal H_S$, the system observables $A_k$ are inserted at their respective time-positions in the Kraus product, and the sum runs over all observation outcomes $i_{1:N}$ with $N \ge j_n$.

For the **alternating pattern** $(j_1, j_2, j_1)$ relevant to Syracuse's row (d), the expectation becomes:
$$ \mathbb E[A^{(j_1)} \cdot B^{(j_2)} \cdot C^{(j_1)}] = \sum_{i_{1:N}} \mathrm{Tr}\left( \cdots M^{(j_2)} \cdots M^{(j_1)} A \cdot M^{(j_1)} C \cdots B \cdot \rho_0 \cdots \right) $$

**Critical structural feature.** In the Kraus product, the system observable $A^{(j_1)}$ at step $j_1$ is sandwiched between the Kraus operators at step $j_1$ — but it is **multiplied** with subsequent Kraus operators $M^{(j_2)}, \ldots$ in the trace, **not** time-translated. The two appearances of "step $j_1$" in $(j_1, j_2, j_1)$ are NOT two physical time-instants; they are **two operator-position-labels in a single time-ordered product**.

This is the Heisenberg-picture analog: $A^{(j)}$ at "time $j$" really means $U_j^* U_{j-1}^* \cdots U_1^* (A \otimes I) U_1 \cdots U_j$, the evolved system observable at time $j$. The product $A^{(j_1)} B^{(j_2)} A^{(j_1)}$ then becomes a non-trivial operator product on $\mathcal H_S \otimes \mathcal H_B^{\otimes N}$, with the two $A^{(j_1)}$ factors NOT in general commuting with $B^{(j_2)}$.

## 3. Comparison with Syracuse's structure

| Belavkin | Syracuse |
|---|---|
| System Hilbert space $\mathcal H_S$ | $\mathcal H = L^2((\mathbb Z/3^n)^*, \pi_n)$ |
| Bath/observation Hilbert space $\mathcal H_B$ | $\ell^2(\mathbb N_{\ge 1})$ — the Geom(2) sample space for $v_j$ |
| Observation algebra $\mathcal B_t$ (abelian) | $\mathbb B_j = vN(M_{b_{[1, k]}} : k \le j)$ — **abelian** ✓ |
| System algebra $\mathcal A_t$ (non-commutative) | $\mathcal A$ generated by $\{T_j\}$ — **non-commutative** ✓ |
| Non-demolition $[X(s), Y(t)] = 0$ | Syracuse's $T_j$ commutes with all $M_{b_{[1, k]}}$ for $k \ne j$ (different probability-space coordinates); for $k = j$, $T_j$ involves shift on $\xi$ AND coupling to $v_j$, so commutativity status is the load-bearing question |
| Kraus operators $M_i^{(j)} = \langle i | U_j | 0\rangle$ — **level-graded** ✓ | Syracuse's per-step transfer operator factors $T_j$ — **level-graded** via $\chi_j(b_{[1,j-1]})$ |
| Adaptive Kraus $M_i^{(j, i_{1:j-1})}$ | Syracuse $T_j = T_j(b_{[1, j-1]})$ — **adaptive** ✓ |
| Multi-time expectation $\mathrm{Tr}(\cdots A_2 M^{(j_2)} A_1 M^{(j_1)} \rho_0 \cdots)$ | Syracuse $\varphi(\tilde X_{j_1} \tilde X_{j_2} \tilde X_{j_1}) = \mathbb E_{\mathbb B}[\tilde X_{j_1} \tilde X_{j_2} \tilde X_{j_1}]$ |

**Key positive observation.** Belavkin's adaptive/level-graded Kraus form $M_i^{(j, i_{1:j-1})}$ matches Syracuse's $T_j(b_{[1, j-1]})$ exactly — both have:
1. The operator at step $j$ is a function of $j$ (level-graded).
2. The operator at step $j$ further depends on the cumulative past observations (adaptive).
3. The observation filtration is abelian.
4. The system algebra is non-commutative.

This is **structurally different from AFL**, where the embeddings $j_t : \mathcal O \to \mathcal A$ were forced to be *-homomorphism transports of a FIXED algebra $\mathcal O$. Belavkin's $M_i^{(j, i_{1:j-1})}$ has no such constraint — each $U_j$ is an independent unitary, so $M_i^{(j)}$ at different $j$ live in $\mathcal B(\mathcal H_S)$ but are NOT related by any time-translation.
